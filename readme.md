# Support for Multi-UAV Integrated Sensors and Communications: Joint UAV Layout and Power Control [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction  
Integrated Sensors and Communications (ISAC) has become an important application scenario for the sixth-generation (6 G) wireless networks [1] and [2]. ISAC aims to utilize existing wireless infrastructure and spectrum/power resources to perform both sensor and communication tasks simultaneously, which is expected to significantly enhance spectrum, energy, and hardware utilization efficiency [3]. In practical applications, radar perception tasks in ISAC systems highly rely on the line-of-sight (LoS) link between the ISAC base station (BS) and the target area to be sensed, and this performance can be severely degraded due to obstacles such as buildings and trees. Therefore, traditional ground-based ISAC BS may only provide high-quality sensing services within limited coverage areas.
To overcome this limitation, adopting UAVs as a new form of airborne ISAC BS becomes an attractive solution. By leveraging the characteristics of high altitude and high mobility, airborne ISAC BS supported by UAVs can establish LoS links with the sensing targets from the sky, providing flexible sensing and communication services [4]-[6]. This enhances the performance of wireless sensing and increases the multifunctionality of wireless communication.
Several previous works have investigated UAV-enabled ISAC networks [7]-[12]. For example, [7] and [8] studied an ISAC system supported by drones, where the mobility and beamforming/precoding of the drone are jointly optimized to maximize the communication rate for multiple ground users (GUs), while ensuring the directional pattern constraints for sensing. Moreover, [9] proposed optimizing the trajectory and resource allocation of the drone to maximize the average communication rate and minimize the Cram 'er-Rao bound (CRB) of the target position estimate, which is constrained by the drone's flight and power limitations. However, these previous works [7]-[9] only considered the case with a single UAV, which may lead to limited coverage range and compromised performance due to the size, weight, and power constraints of the UAV.
Multi-UAV Collaborative ISAC is a feasible solution to the above problems. It allows multiple UAVs to collaborate in serving multiple GUs and sense the ground target areas, achieving improved communication and sensing performance. On one hand, a group of UAVs can cooperate to provide seamless coverage over a large area, coordinating signals with minimal interference transmission. On the other hand, multiple UAVs can collaborate in distributed radar sensing, with different perception angles and enhanced perception capabilities [12]. However, the presence of multiple UAVs introduces more complex interference between the sensed and information signals at multiple UAV locations. Therefore, how to achieve an optimal balance between sensor and communication performance across multiple UAVs has become a challenging task. This motivates us to study joint UAV layout and power control in this work to optimize the performance of multi-UAV enabled ISAC.
A multi-UAV collaborative ISAC system was studied, where multiple single-antenna UAVs send information to their respective single-antenna GUs while also detecting ground targets within the interest area using the information signal. Under this setup, our goal is to maximize the minimum detection probability in the target area through joint optimization of the UAV layout position and transmit power control, while satisfying the individual minimum Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise-Ratio (SINR) constraints at the GU, the maximum transmit power constraint for each UAV, and the minimum UAV inter-distance constraint for avoidance. Firstly, we studied the joint optimization problem for two UAVs and a point target. We obtained closed-form solutions for both scenarios of only perception and ISAC. Secondly, considering the general case, we proposed an iterative algorithm based on successive convex approximation to obtain high-quality solutions. Finally, numerical results demonstrated the effectiveness of the method. These results indicate that the proposed solution has advantages in enhancing the perception and communication performance of multi-UAV supported ISAC.
Conclusion
This study investigates a multi-UAV collaborative ISAC system, aiming to maximize the minimum detection probability in the target area while ensuring the SINR at GU is constrained to the minimum. The joint optimization problem of two UAVs and a point target was first considered, leading to a closed-form solution for this problem, balancing between communication and perception. Based on SCA, a generalized iterative algorithm was proposed. Numerical results show that the proposed iterative algorithm significantly improves system performance compared to the baseline scheme.
